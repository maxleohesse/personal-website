About

Who I am

I grew up in Essen and now study International Business in Maastricht. Somewhere between those two things I worked out what I actually enjoy: getting people into a room who wouldn’t otherwise meet, and building something with them.

That became MAIN, the Maastricht AI & Tech Network, which I co-founded and now lead. We started it on a simple observation: AI is going to reshape every field, and yet students in law, medicine and business were never talking about it with each other. So we opened a door. It’s now a community of 300+ students, run by a team of 26.

What I’m working on

Most of my attention right now goes to the MAIN Summit — two days in November where we want to bring 300 to 500 students from across Europe to Maastricht for a hackathon, keynotes, and a lot of unplanned conversation. We’re still looking for speakers and partners who want to be part of it.

The harder question sits underneath that one, and it’s quieter: how do you lead 26 people without becoming a hierarchy, and how do you build something that outlives the people who started it? I’d like to come back in five years and find MAIN still here, bigger than we managed.

Where this is going

I don’t know exactly, and I’ve stopped pretending otherwise.

What pulls me: small teams, early-stage things, being close to people who are better than me at what I want to learn. I’d like to get more technical than a business degree makes you — I’d rather understand a thing than manage it from a distance. And I still consume far more than I create, which is a large part of why I started writing here.
